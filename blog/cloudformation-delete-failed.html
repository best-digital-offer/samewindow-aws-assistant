CloudFormation DELETE_FAILED: How to Investigate Before Retrying

Diagnose CloudFormation DELETE_FAILED states by checking resource dependencies, permissions, retained data, and service-specific blockers.

A DELETE_FAILED status means CloudFormation could not remove at least one resource. The failed resource’s event usually explains the immediate blocker. Repeating deletion without addressing that reason often produces the same result.

Quick summary: Capture the exact error, confirm the account and Region, identify the layer that failed, and verify a minimal fix. Do not share AWS credentials, session tokens, or secrets with a troubleshooting tool.

Read the failed resource event

Open stack events and find the resource that failed deletion. Review the status reason and the resource’s current state in its service console. The reason may point to non-empty storage, dependent resources, permissions, or a service-specific constraint.

Check data retention requirements

Buckets, databases, volumes, and other resources may contain data that must be retained or backed up. Confirm the resource’s lifecycle and retention requirements with the owner before emptying or deleting anything.

Practical tip: Change one thing at a time, keep the original error, and avoid sharing credentials, tokens, or unredacted production data.

Verify dependency and permission constraints

CloudFormation needs permission to delete the resource, and the service may require other conditions to be satisfied. Check dependent resources, resource policies, deletion protection, and any organization-level restrictions.

Use recovery options deliberately

Some failed stacks can be retried after the blocker is removed. Other situations require documented recovery options or retaining specific resources. Understand how any skipped resource may leave drift or an unmanaged resource behind.

Confirm final ownership

After recovery, verify whether the resource was deleted, retained, or left outside stack management. Update documentation and inventory so future deployments do not assume CloudFormation still owns it.

Frequently asked questions

Is emptying a bucket always required?

No. It depends on the failed resource and the data-retention decision. Never remove production data merely to make stack deletion succeed.

What does retaining a resource mean?

The resource can remain after stack removal or recovery, depending on the operation. It must still have an owner and a documented lifecycle.
