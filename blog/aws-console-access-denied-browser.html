How to Fix AWS Console Access Denied Errors in Your Browser

Troubleshoot AWS Console access-denied errors by checking IAM policies, network restrictions, browser extensions, and session conditions safely.

An AWS Console access-denied message can come from permissions, organization guardrails, resource policies, session conditions, or network restrictions. The fastest route to a useful diagnosis is to capture the exact message and identify which layer denied the request before changing permissions.

Quick summary: Capture the exact error, confirm the account and Region, identify the layer that failed, and verify a minimal fix. Do not share AWS credentials, session tokens, or secrets with a troubleshooting tool.

Start with the exact error text

Copy the complete message, including the action, resource, and any explanation of the policy type. A message such as “not authorized to perform” is more useful than the words “Access denied” alone. Do not paste credentials, session tokens, or private customer data into troubleshooting tools.

Check identity-based and resource-based policies

Confirm the signed-in principal and the requested action. Review identity policies and, where applicable, the bucket, key, queue, or other resource policy. Cross-account access commonly requires permission on both sides. An allow statement in one policy does not override an applicable explicit deny.

Practical tip: Change one thing at a time, keep the original error, and avoid sharing credentials, tokens, or unredacted production data.

Inspect organization and session guardrails

Service control policies, resource control policies, permissions boundaries, session policies, and role trust policies can restrict an action even when an identity policy appears to allow it. Use the error’s policy-type clues and your organization’s approved access-review process.

Rule out browser and network causes

If the error is in the console UI rather than an authorization response, test a private window and another supported browser. Temporarily disable extensions only in accordance with company policy. Proxies, content filters, VPC endpoint policies, and IPv4/IPv6 source-IP conditions can also affect console access.

Verify the fix with least privilege

Ask an administrator to make the smallest policy change that permits the required action and resource. Retest the original operation and review CloudTrail where appropriate. Avoid attaching AdministratorAccess simply to make an error disappear.

Frequently asked questions

Does AccessDenied always mean an IAM policy is missing?

No. It can also result from explicit denies, resource policies, organization policies, permissions boundaries, session policies, trust policies, endpoint policies, or a console/network issue.

Should I add AdministratorAccess to fix it?

No. Identify the denied action and policy layer first, then apply least-privilege access through your normal review process.
