AWS STS AssumeRole Access Denied: Trust Policy vs Permissions

Understand AssumeRole failures by separating role trust relationships, caller permissions, session conditions, and organization guardrails.

An STS AssumeRole failure often confuses two distinct questions: is the caller allowed to request the role, and does the target role trust that caller? Both sides must be evaluated, along with conditions and organization controls.

Quick summary: Capture the exact error, confirm the account and Region, identify the layer that failed, and verify a minimal fix. Do not share AWS credentials, session tokens, or secrets with a troubleshooting tool.

Identify the caller and target role

Capture the caller’s account and principal type, the target role ARN, and the exact STS error. Confirm that the role exists in the expected account and that the request uses the intended external ID, session name, and other required parameters.

Review the caller’s permission to assume the role

The caller commonly needs an identity-based policy allowing sts:AssumeRole on the target role ARN. Check conditions and any explicit denies or organizational restrictions.

Practical tip: Change one thing at a time, keep the original error, and avoid sharing credentials, tokens, or unredacted production data.

Review the target role trust policy

The target role’s trust policy controls which principals may assume it. Confirm that the principal and any condition keys match the intended identity and request context. Cross-account setups require particular attention to both accounts’ configuration.

Check MFA, external ID, and session constraints

A trust policy may require MFA, an external ID, source identity, or other conditions. The request must satisfy them. Do not remove a security condition simply because a request fails; verify the intended trust model with the role owner.

Retest and audit

Use the approved identity and exact role to repeat the request, then review relevant CloudTrail records. Document whether the failure was caller authorization, trust, or another guardrail.

Frequently asked questions

Does the target role’s trust policy grant all permissions?

No. Trust determines who can assume the role; permissions attached to the role govern what the resulting session can do.

Can I fix AssumeRole by adding AdministratorAccess to the caller?

Not necessarily. A trust-policy mismatch or explicit deny can still block the request, and broad access is not a sound default fix.
